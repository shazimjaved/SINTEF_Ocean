Task 1 Problem Definition and Business Understanding
SINTEF Ocean Real Vessel Data Challenge | TDT4259 Applied Data Science
25 October 2026 | Preliminary technical findings
1. Problem and practical objective
The objective is to estimate vessel propeller shaft mechanical power (kW) from operating and environmental
measurements that do not originate from the same torquemeter channel. Such an estimate could provide a
secondary indication of propulsion demand or help check whether a measured power value is plausible. Its
usefulness for performance monitoring will depend on the predictive accuracy and the availability of input signals
when an estimate is needed.
The task is formulated as supervised regression. The target is the recorded shaft mechanical power, and each
training example represents an aligned one-minute period. The present formulation is a completed-minute power
estimate: measurements within the minute are summarized at the end of that minute. It is not a forecast
of the following minute, an instantaneous prediction at the beginning of the minute, or a demonstrated
route-optimization system.
The target is identified in the registration material as Vessel Propeller Shaft Mechanical Power. Its registered
QID is:
2::0::11::0_1::1::0::3::0_14::0::1::0_8
It occurs in the twelve monthly 2024 datasets but not in the January 2025 file. January 2025 therefore cannot
provide labelled evaluation observations for this target.

The 24 candidate channels comprise four engine measurements from other named sources, four motion
channels, two onboard relative-wind channels and fourteen environmental provider channels. Relevant examples
include engine RPM, turbocharger RPM, scavenging air pressure, fuel load, speed over ground, speed through
water, wind and wave conditions, and sea-water current components.
A measurement’s presence in the dataset does not by itself establish that it will be available at the intended
operational decision time. The engine-source timing and weather-provider issuance times require confirmation.
Angular variables such as heading and wind direction also require circular processing rather than naïve arithmetic
averaging.
3. Data characteristics affecting the objective
Target reliability.

including a near-contiguous sequence of 17,399 readings. November contains 656 negative readings, with a
minimum near 24,757 kW. These patterns create a substantial risk of unreliable labels. Although the challenge

Observation density. September and October supply 74.9% and 49.5%, respectively, of the target readings

Environmental measurements. In October, the provider channels have hourly source observations. Although

369 distinct provider timestamps. Repeated aligned weather values must not be interpreted as independent
observations.
Representativeness. Vessel loading, operating regimes, environmental conditions and repeated measurements
can change observed relationships. Consequently, the October speed–power association is not evidence of a
vessel-wide resistance law.

The roles above define a proposed evaluation, not completed experiments. The December test period includes
166 negative target readings. Any quality-filtered evaluation must use rules fixed without looking at test
prediction errors, and the number of excluded cases must be reported. July and November remain outside the
primary fitting and headline testing periods unless their reliability can be established.
5. Success criteria and expected outputs
Model evaluation will use mean absolute error (MAE) and root mean squared error (RMSE) in kW,
with R2 reported as a supplementary measure. Results should include observation counts, performance by time
block or operating condition, and comparison with appropriately defined simple baselines: a training-mean
estimate, a speed-based heuristic fitted only on training data and, when genuinely available, a persistence
estimate based on a previously trusted power reading.
The immediate technical criterion is a meaningful improvement over suitable baselines on untouched chronological
data. A satisfactory operational error tolerance cannot yet be fixed without a decision context and stakeholder-
defined requirements. Claims of fuel savings, route improvements or operational deployment must wait for
measured predictive performance and a realistic assessment of input availability.
6. Working assumptions and next decisions
The most consequential open decisions are the reliability boundaries of the torquemeter, the issue-time and
timezone meaning of environmental data, the latency of onboard engine measurements, and whether a lagged
Noon Report loading indicator would genuinely be available at inference time. These are explicit conditions on
the eventual model design, not evidence of achieved model performance.
