1. Dataset inventory
The dataset contains 41,079,968 sensor records in twelve 2024 monthly files and a further 1,112,405
records for January 2025, giving 42,192,373 records across thirteen files. The sensor registration identifies
37 QIDs. Thirty-six occur in the 2024 data; 26 occur in January 2025. The registered channel without observed
values is a turbocharger RPM indicator. All torquemeter channels, including the shaft-power target, are absent
in January 2025, so that month is not a labelled test period.
The quality scan parsed all 42,192,373 observed sensor values as finite numeric values and found no malformed
short records or duplicate original (timestamp, QID) keys. Recorded numeric completeness should not be
confused with coverage across calendar time.

channels with constant zero readings, one water-depth channel without October target overlap, and 24 initially
eligible predictors. The predictors comprise four other-source engine, four vessel-motion, two onboard
relative-wind, and 14 environmental provider channels.

3.1 Target-power anomalies
July 2024. The shaft-power series contains 28,544 negative readings across fourteen dates. All target
readings on 11, 12 and 15 July are negative. The longest near-contiguous negative sequence spans 17,399
readings. At completed-minute resolution, 6,948 negative-power intervals also coincide with positive CAMS
engine RPM and speed through water above 3 knots.
November 2024. There are 656 negative readings across eight dates, with a minimum near 24,757 kW.
The combined negative-power/positive-engine-operation condition occurs in 93 one-minute intervals.
These patterns support a target-quality concern and separate assessment of July and November. They do
not on their own distinguish calibration errors from reversal, sign convention or other operating circumstances.
Negative observations have not been silently clipped or replaced. June has no negative target readings, but that
absence alone is not evidence of clipping.

The strong engine associations indicate that these signals may be informative for reconstructing shaft power,
subject to availability and independence checks. The weather correlations use repeated provider values and
serially related target minutes; 22,088 paired minutes are not equivalent to 22,088 independent weather
observations. These associations are not causal effects or trained-model feature importances.
The October speed–power relationship is also not consistently increasing. Among nonnegative speed and power
intervals, the observed median decreases from approximately 2,997 kW at 7.0–7.5 knots to 2,564 kW at
10.0–10.5 knots and 2,431 kW at 11.0–11.5 knots. An unconstrained descriptive cubic fit likewise falls from
approximately 2,955 kW at 7 knots to 2,576 kW at 10 knots. In contrast, the familiar idealized power speed3
expression is a physical heuristic under assumptions that these pooled observations do not satisfy. Loading,
operating conditions, time effects and measurement quality have not been controlled sufficiently to explain the
empirical decline.

5. Noon Reports and implications for modeling
The twelve Noon Report datasets cover all 366 unique dates in leap year 2024. Their daily cadence is much
coarser than sensor records. June includes missing fuel type and propulsion-consumption fields; 18 extreme
slip entries were flagged during the year, including 60% and +50% values. Daily displacement could act as a
loading indicator, but a same-day measurement may not have been available early on that date. Incorporating it
requires documented measurement and issuance times or a defensible lag.
The provisional chronological design is January–June training, August validation and December final
testing. July and November are reserved for quality investigation, while September–October provide coverage-
stress analysis. December’s 166 negative target readings require an evaluation policy established before
inspecting final test errors.
6. Interpretation and limitations
The current findings support a coherent starting point for regression, but they do not establish predictive accuracy,
fuel savings or deployment readiness. The main dependencies are the torquemeter’s trustworthy operating
periods, provider timestamp semantics, engine-signal availability and the generalizability of relationships across
voyages and loading conditions. A completed-minute reconstruction should not be described as a real-time
forecast without further evidence.
