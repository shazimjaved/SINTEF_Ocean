Task 1 — Problem Definition and Business Understanding

Version 2 · 30 October 2026 · Business scope and decision requirements
1. Problem and practical objective
The business problem is to determine whether the vessel requires more propulsion power, or achieves less speed,
than a defensible clean-condition speed–power reference would indicate under comparable operating conditions. A
credible comparison could help the shipowner's performance and maintenance teams decide whether to
investigate a performance change, arrange an inspection, consider hull or propeller cleaning, or defer action while
obtaining better evidence.
This framing follows the challenge's clean- and current-condition baseline objectives and its request to estimate
added power or speed loss. The SINTEF presentation explicitly asks whether performance indicates a need for hull
and/or propeller cleaning and, additionally, whether cleaning is worthwhile. Fuel costs and operational efficiency
motivate the work, but the value of a specific maintenance action must be assessed separately. [C1, C2, C3]
The intended comparison is conditional: speed, loading, trim, environmental conditions and operating state must
be comparable, and both the reference and observed power must be trustworthy. A difference is initially a possible
excess-power or speed-loss indication. It does not establish fouling or identify its location. Sensor problems,
reference mismatch and changing operating conditions remain alternative explanations.
Shaft-power estimation remains an enabling capability. It may provide a secondary indication of propulsion
demand, support a plausibility check or help address missing observations where justified. An estimate that
reproduces current power is not automatically an estimate of clean-condition power. The reference condition
requires its own evidence. Technical details preserved from the original delivery are recorded in Task 2 v2, Appendix
A. [R1, R2, A1]
2. Business users and supported decisions
The following are intended user roles; specific people, responsibilities and acceptance thresholds must be agreed
with the project stakeholders.
Intended user
Supported decision and intended output
Shipowner / vessel performance team
Review a dated, uncertainty-qualified comparison of observed demand with an applicable
reference; decide whether the indication warrants investigation.
Technical superintendent /
maintenance planner
Combine performance evidence with inspection and maintenance history to consider
inspection, cleaning or deferral.
Vessel engineers / operational staff
Check measurement reliability, loading changes and operating circumstances that could
explain a performance indication.
Operations / commercial decision
owner
Assess scheduling and economic implications once cleaning scope, cost, downtime and
credible benefit evidence are available.
The supported output is a documented basis for maintenance review. Cleaning remains a human decision informed
by operational evidence; this report does not recommend a particular cleaning event.
