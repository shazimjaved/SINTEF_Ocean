3. Data characteristics affecting the objective
The package contains operational sensor data, daily Noon Reports and engineering references, but the evidence has
important limits. The delivered data analysis identifies suspect shaft-power readings, uneven coverage, unusable
sensor draft values, repeated weather observations and uncertain timestamp semantics. These issues can make an
apparent performance change unreliable. A single vessel and changing loading, weather and operating states also
constrain generalization. Task 2 retains the numerical findings and their qualifications. [R2]
Actual hull/propeller cleaning-event records were not found in the inspected package. The available towing-tank
report explicitly predicts performance with a clean bottom under specified conditions, but this does not prove that
any dated interval in the observed sensor series represents a clean vessel. The sea-trial image also needs fuller
provenance before use as a validated reference. An actual engine shop-test report or fuel/load map was not found
in the inspected package. [A1, A2]
4. Scope and staged decision process
The project should first establish reference applicability, trustworthy observation periods and comparable operating
conditions. It can then assess whether an interpretable performance comparison is feasible and, if supported,
present possible excess power or speed loss with uncertainty for maintenance review. This revision defines that
decision process; it does not calculate the comparison.
The original completed-minute shaft-power reconstruction remains a supporting use. Advance route planning
requires a separately defined decision horizon and inputs available at that time. Distinguishing hull from propeller
fouling is optional in the original challenge and requires additional evidence. Estimating fuel savings or deciding
whether cleaning is economically worthwhile is conditional on validated performance and cost information. [C1, C3,
R1]
5. Success criteria and expected outputs
Success should be judged by whether the evidence supports a useful and appropriately qualified decision. The
following principles are proposed for agreement with stakeholders; no numerical acceptance threshold or achieved
benefit is asserted.
Success-measure principle
What the eventual output should make assessable
Reference and condition coverage
The share of relevant operating periods with trustworthy observations and an applicable
reference at comparable conditions; unsupported periods remain explicit.
Decision-relevant uncertainty
Whether uncertainty is small enough relative to an agreed maintenance-review threshold to
justify investigation or action.
Reliability of review indications
How often a flagged change is subsequently explained by measurement or operating
conditions, and how that affects confidence in future reviews.
Usefulness to the decision owner
Whether performance staff can document a supported inspect, consider-cleaning or defer
decision, with evidence and unresolved questions visible.
Intended outputs are a reference-applicability statement, a clearly dated comparison window, a qualified
power/speed indication where defensible, and a concise maintenance-review brief. Technical prediction accuracy is
an enabling quality measure, not a substitute for these decision requirements. No fouling detection, accuracy result,
cleaning saving, fuel saving or cost-benefit estimate is demonstrated in this revision.
