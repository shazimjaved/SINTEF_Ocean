Strong engine associations suggest potential information for power reconstruction, subject to availability and
independence checks. Weather correlations reuse hourly values and serially related target minutes: 22,088 pairs are not
22,088 independent weather observations; only 369 distinct provider timestamps contribute. These associations are
neither causal effects nor trained-model feature importances. [R2]
The October speed–power relation is not consistently increasing. Among nonnegative speed and power intervals, median
power decreases from approximately 2,997 kW at 7.0–7.5 kn to 2,564 kW at 10.0–10.5 kn and 2,431 kW at 11.0–11.5
kn. The existing unconstrained descriptive cubic falls from approximately 2,955 kW at 7 kn to 2,576 kW at 10 kn. These
retained observations do not establish the vessel's resistance law. [R2]
The idealized relationship of power proportional to speed cubed is a physical heuristic under assumptions not
established for these pooled observations. Loading, operating conditions, time/voyage effects and measurement quality
have not been controlled sufficiently to explain the empirical decline. Regime mixing is not established as its cause.
Figure 6 shows the illustrative theoretical shape separately from observed summaries. [R2, M1]
Implication for the business question: this EDA supplies neither a clean/current label nor an estimate attributable to
fouling. A future comparison needs an applicable reference and matched or justified corrections for speed, loading, trim,
environment and operating state, with uncertainty carried through. No clean/current comparison is produced here. [C1,
C3, A1]
