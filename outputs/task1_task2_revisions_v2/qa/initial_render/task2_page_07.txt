5. Noon Reports and implications for modeling
The twelve Noon Report CSVs contain 366 valid unique dates, covering every date in leap year 2024. Their daily cadence
is much coarser than sensor records. Forward, mid and aft draft and displacement are populated on all 366 dates and
could support coarse loading descriptions. The supplied column headings do not confirm their units. The particulars
workbook explicitly reports displacement volume in m³, so equivalence to Noon displacement must not be assumed.
Draft definitions, trim derivation, units and any density conversion require confirmation. [R2, N1, V1, A1]
Observation and issuance times are unknown. A same-day value may not have been available early that day or represent
loading throughout it. A defensible lag may address online availability, but can misrepresent loading changes;
retrospective comparison also needs evidence of the loading applicable to the sensor interval. [R2, A1]
The existing audit flags 18 extreme slip entries, including −60% and +50%; these are review flags, not cleaning indicators.
The scope audit found 116 blank propulsion-consumption entries and 116 blank fuel-type entries across the year,
including all 30 June dates. Blank values do not imply zero consumption. One loading item requiring review is the 31
March record: forward draft 5, mid 5.93, aft 1.29 and displacement 34015. Its definitions/transcription require checking
before trim or reference matching; it is not declared erroneous here. [R2, N1, A1]
There is no cleaning-event field or cleaning-related entry in the inspected Noon CSVs. The original month split has been
relocated to Appendix A to preserve its historical specification while keeping this section data-oriented. [A1, A2]
6. Interpretation and limitations
The package offers candidate engineering reference material and substantial operational observations, but it does not
establish a dated clean state in the sensor series. Cleaning-event records and an actual engine shop-test report/map
were not found in the inspected package. Cleaning-event coverage, completeness, timing precision, component/extent
and post-clean condition cannot be assessed without those records. Promised data and example clean flags do not
resolve this absence. [A1, A2]
Reference use requires the correct PSV configuration and loading/trim, an appropriate speed range, comparable
environment/depth and operating state, compatible power definitions/measurement location, documented corrections
and uncertainty. The towing report assumes calm sea, sufficient depth and a clean bottom. Its with/without-PSV
percentages are not cleaning savings. The trial chart's 8833 and 0.985 annotations and the towing report's 9500 kW
rating and 0.99 transmission efficiency require reconciliation; their difference alone does not establish a vessel
mismatch. [V2, V3, A1]
Requests needed to close these gaps are dated cleaning/inspection and maintenance history; reference/trial provenance
and numeric curves; calibration/operation logs; loading, weather and timestamp metadata; and a defined
current-condition window and decision horizon. These requests support feasibility assessment, not an assertion that
fouling has occurred. [A1]
The retained descriptive findings do not establish predictive accuracy, fuel savings, cleaning effectiveness or deployment
readiness. Provider timing, engine-signal availability, loading representativeness and reliable target periods remain open.
Completed-minute reconstruction does not establish a real-time or advance route-planning forecast. The pending Task 3
work has not been assessed in this revision. [R2]
