# Task 3 V5: physical references and retrospective loading diagnostics

This is an internal investigation, not an operational model or a client report. V1–V4 artifacts and raw records were left unchanged. No model was trained. All empirical comparisons use the existing one-minute tables for January–October 2024; December was not read. July is descriptive only because its target remains quarantined. Illustrative template CSVs were not used.

## Source-verified physical references

- Visual inspection of `docs/vessel/GA.jpg` gives a MAN B&W 5S60MC-C Mark VII-tier II main engine at **8,833 kW × 105 rpm**. `docs/vessel/Sea_Trials_curves.jpg` uses 8,833 kW in its DHP annotations (8,833 × 0.985 ≈ 8,701 kW). The towing report PDF page 8 instead specifies **Wartsila 5RT-flex58T-D, 9,500 kW × 105 rpm**, transmission efficiency 0.99, and propeller TM1747A (6.8 m, four blades, pitch ratio 0.6742). `Particulars.xlsx` row 71 also gives 9,500 kW × 105 rpm and rows 75–80 match those propeller particulars. The ratings differ by **667 kW (7.55% of 8,833 kW)** and engine types differ. Shared hull dimensions alone do not prove the references describe an interchangeable propulsion installation.
- The challenge presentation slide 4 says the vessel has a pre-shrouded vane (PSV). The towing PDF pages 24, 32, 40, 48 contain with-PSV speed-power Tables **7, 13, 19, 25**, respectively. All **36 numerical rows**, including speed, effective power, corrected delivered power at the propeller (PDT), propeller rpm and reported efficiency terms, are in `tow_tank_with_psv_tables.csv`. Design and scantling cover 11.5–15.5 and 11–15 kn; ballast and heavy ballast cover 12–16 kn. These are predictions for **calm sea, sufficient water depth and clean bottom**, at four specified drafts and displacement *volumes* (m³), not measurements at arbitrary operating states.
- Two source contradictions are retained rather than silently fixed. PDF Table 25's title says “WITHOUT PSV”, while the same page lists PSV as an appendage and the with-PSV summary on PDF page 14 reproduces its values. The primary ballast Table 19 lists **4,042 kW at 13 kn**, while the PDF page 13 summary lists **4,041 kW**. The extracted CSV uses primary Table 19 and flags both caveats in `tow_tank_source_summary.csv`.
- The measured target is registered as **shaft mechanical power, Instrument Torquemeter, kW**. The towing-tank variable is corrected **delivered power at the propeller**. The report uses a load-dependent correction factor CP (0.96 for design/scantling, 1.0 for ballast cases) and states a 0.99 transmission efficiency; the sea-trial graph annotates a 0.985 factor. The physical location of the torquemeter relative to the propeller, its calibration, and the correct conversion convention are unavailable. Accordingly, no direct equality, efficiency correction or target calibration is inferred from target/PDT ratios. Sea trials are plotted curves without a numerical source table and were not digitized as measured observations.

## Displacement unit evidence and timing

The August Noon CSV header says only `Displacement`, without a unit or issuance time. Its **31 daily values are all 89,145**. The scantling reference volume is **86,941 m³**; assuming seawater density **1.025 t/m³** gives **89,114.525 t**, only **30.475 t (0.0342%)** below the Noon number. The August Noon mid draft median is **14.2 m**, close to the 14.25 m scantling reference, though its fore/aft trim differs. This is strong evidence that the Noon field may represent tonnes, but its unit is **not source verified**. A Noon data dictionary or vessel hydrostatic record is needed to establish it. All Noon joins here are retrospective by calendar date, with an end-labelled minute assigned to its covered date by subtracting one minute. No date-only Noon field was added to model inputs.

## What the empirical comparisons show

- Under the explicit diagnostic proximity rule (fore/aft draft scales 2 m, displacement scale 15,000 assumed tonnes, Euclidean distance ≤1), **all 31 August dates are scantling-like; January–June has no scantling-like date**. The earlier development data therefore provide no close loading analogue for August. The rule is descriptive and depends on the unverified Noon unit.
- August has **35,086 positive forward-power minutes, 6,030 recorded-zero forward minutes and 89 negative/mixed forward minutes**. The zero counts remain separate. The reference curves cover only **19,779** August positive minutes at their specified speed ranges and close loading. Recorded zeros were never divided by a theoretical reference or labelled faulty.
- The same Noon displacement value **89,145** spans the August 27 zero onset and all four fully zero-power days, August 28–31 (1,440 forward zero minutes per day in the compact minute table). The date-only load proxy cannot identify those shaft-zero states. This does not establish whether the target or propulsion state is wrong.
- At 12.0–12.5 kn under the provisional scantling category, August has **4,165 positive minutes, median 4,249 kW**; September has **5,303 positive minutes, median 5,041 kW**, plus **1,110 zero-power minutes** in that speed bin. The same reported displacement (89,145) and similar draft do not remove the positive-target shift. Weather, engine state, target behavior and reporting quality remain plausible alternatives. September's reduced target observation density and zero episodes limit this comparison.
- Matching the September positive readings against August positive readings at the same provisional loading, using speed, engine RPM, fuel load and scavenging pressure, yields **1,566 of 1,685** ten-minute September samples within normalized distance 1. The matched median September-minus-August difference is **+119.4 kW** (10th/90th percentiles +11/+276 kW), considerably smaller than the unconditioned 12.0–12.5 kn bin contrast. These pairs use only **112 distinct August peers**, exclude zeros and may select different operating intervals. This is consistent with measured engine state contributing to the August–September speed-bin difference; it is not a causal decomposition or a solution to January–June→August generalization.
- Retrospective nearest-neighbour matching on **STW, engine RPM, fuel load and scavenging pressure**, sampled every ten minutes, found **3,064 of 3,517** August positive samples within the predeclared normalized distance of 1 of earlier positive samples. Their median August-minus-earlier power is **−899.6 kW** (10th/90th percentiles −1,219/−436 kW). Those matches use only **433 distinct earlier peers** and **none** has the same provisional loading category; median Noon displacement is 89,145 versus 35,885. This confirms a conditional target discrepancy, but loading and period remain confounded. It does not identify calibration as the cause.
- Reference normalization does not collapse periods. Median of eligible daily positive-power/reference-PDT ratios (at least 30 in-range minutes/day) is **1.269** for earlier ballast-like dates (41 days), **1.353** for earlier design-like dates (49 days), **0.954** in August (21 days), and **1.033** for later scantling-like September dates (13 days). These are diagnostics only: weather, hull condition, speed measurement, engine/propeller specification and shaft-versus-propeller power definitions differ from the calm-water reference. The August change is not explained by a simple loading normalization, and the shaft-zero episodes require separate status evidence.

## Next experiment to predeclare before fitting

First obtain (1) a Noon field dictionary with displacement units and measurement/issuance timestamps; (2) shaft sensor location, torque scale, calibration and quality/event logs; and (3) actual PSV/propeller and propulsion-line configuration for the observed vessel. Then predeclare a **development-only** ablation comparing current label-free onboard baselines with an explicit loading/reference feature set, using only loading values demonstrably available before prediction. Evaluate chronological Apr, May, Jun and Aug folds on both positive-target and broader-forward scopes, with overlap and zero-state coverage reported. If timing or source comparability remains unresolved, run the reference only as retrospective sensitivity, not as a ranked deployable feature. Do not use December for selection.
